Why ShalomNY Exists

New York State is home to 1.7 million Jews — more than any country on earth except Israel. If New York were its own country, it would rank third in the world for Jewish population, behind only Israel and the rest of the United States. New York City alone has more Jews than France, more than the United Kingdom, more than Argentina.

In fact, New York's Jewish population is nearly as large as the entire non-Israel, non-US Jewish diaspora combined — larger than France, Canada, the UK, Argentina, Germany, Russia, Australia, Brazil, South Africa, Hungary, and Mexico put together.

It is the largest Jewish community on the planet outside of Israel. And yet, until now, there has been no single place to see where New York's elected officials actually stand on the issues that matter most to this community — Israel policy, antisemitism protections, BDS legislation, and more.

From Congress to the City Council, 292 elected officials make decisions that directly affect Jewish New Yorkers. ShalomNY tracks every one of them.

We monitor public statements, legislative actions, rally attendance, voting records, and social media posts across five legislative bodies — the US Senate, US House, NY State Senate, NY Assembly, and NYC City Council — to build a comprehensive, evidence-based picture of where each representative stands on Israel.

What We Track

Every piece of evidence in our database is sourced from public records: official statements, press releases, legislative votes, news coverage, and verified social media posts. We track 16 specific criteria including:

Legislation

Anti-BDS bills, antisemitism protection acts, settlement-related legislation, and aid policy

Rhetoric

Use of terms like "genocide" or "apartheid," condemnation of Hamas and Oct 7, ceasefire positions

Actions

Rally attendance, protest participation, solidarity missions to Israel, letter co-signatures

Policy

Positions on Iron Dome funding, conditioning US aid, and diplomatic stances

Our Principles

Evidence-based. Every score is derived from documented, sourced public actions — not opinion or hearsay. Each evidence item links to its original source so you can verify it yourself.

Comprehensive. We don't cherry-pick. If a legislator condemned Hamas but also called for a ceasefire, both are recorded. The full picture matters more than any single data point.

Non-partisan. We track Democrats, Republicans, and independents equally. Support for Israel is not a partisan issue — our data shows pro-Israel and anti-Israel voices exist across the political spectrum.

Transparent. Our scoring methodology is open. Each criterion has a weight, a direction (pro or anti), and every evidence item is individually viewable with its source link.

How Scores Work

Each legislator's score is computed from their evidence items. Pro-Israel actions add positive points and anti-Israel actions subtract points, with weights based on significance (e.g., sponsoring legislation weighs more than attending an event). The score reflects the overall direction and intensity of their public record.

Legislators with no public position on Israel receive no score — absence of evidence is not evidence of opposition.
